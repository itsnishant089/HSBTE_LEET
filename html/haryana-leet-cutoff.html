Haryana LEET Cutoff - Rank vs College

What is Haryana LEET Cutoff? (B.Tech)

For B.Tech LEET, cutoff is the last rank / score at which a candidate is admitted to a specific engineering branch in a college for a given category and round. It changes every year based on:

  • Number of applicants in engineering streams.
  • Difficulty level of the B.Tech LEET paper and performance of candidates.
  • Total available B.Tech seats in each college / branch.
  • Category-wise reservation and seat matrix notified by HSTES.

What is Haryana LEET Cutoff? (B.Pharmacy)

For B.Pharmacy LEET, cutoff refers to the last rank / score at which admission is granted to B.Pharm lateral entry seats. Because total pharmacy seats are fewer, closing ranks can behave differently from B.Tech. Cutoff is affected by:

  • Number of D.Pharm candidates applying for B.Pharm LEET.
  • Seat availability in pharmacy colleges approved for LEET intake.
  • Category-wise distribution and reservation policies.

Why Previous Year Cutoff is Important

  • Helps you estimate which colleges are realistically possible for your expected rank.
  • Guides choice filling order during Haryana LEET counselling.
  • Shows which branches are more in demand (CSE, ECE, Mechanical, etc.).
  • Helps you decide a safe target rank for preparation.

Accessing Official Cutoff Data

Official year-wise cutoff lists are usually published on:

  • HSTES official website
  • Websites of participating universities / technical institutes (for reference)

Always download the latest cutoff PDF from official sources for accurate figures.

How to Read Haryana LEET Cutoff PDFs

  • Look for columns such as Institute Name, Branch, Category, Opening Rank, Closing Rank.
  • Check separate lists for different rounds (Round 1, Round 2, Spot Round etc.).
  • Compare multiple years to see if a college is consistently in high demand.

Using Cutoff for 2026 Planning

  • Decide a target rank for 2026 by looking at last year-s closing ranks of your dream colleges.
  • Make a list of:
    • Dream colleges (high cutoff)
    • Medium colleges (reachable)
    • Safe colleges (lower cutoff)
  • During choice filling, mix all three categories instead of selecting only top colleges.
